==== overview
How the model works out its state
the code as it stands

The model has two kinds of state. Whose move a conversation is, and what waits for the agent,
are worked out from the messages each time something asks, and never stored (#1, #2). What you
have read, walked and accepted is kept in <id>.seen beside the review, under keys the model
makes (#3, then the keys in #4 and #5). Each thread answers one question with a tour you walk
with L and H.

==== #1 whole
◇ #1  Whose move a conversation is   HOW IT WORKS 

    Whose move it is comes from the last message, every time something asks. If your message
    is last, it is the agent's move. If the agent's is last and you have said something in
    that conversation, it is yours. If only the agent has spoken, or the thread is closed,
    it is nobody's move. None of this is stored.

    A conversation is either a thread's own messages (#12) or the messages of one of its
    steps (#12.3). Each conversation has its own move. When you reply in a step, that step
    becomes the agent's move and the thread's own conversation does not change.

    A worked case, which I ran in a scratch repository. Thread 1's introduction is the
    agent's, and it carries a tour of two steps. At first #1, #1.1 and #1.2 are all nobody's
    move. You reply in step 1, and then:

    - #1 still holds only the agent's introduction: nobody's move;
    - #1.1 holds your reply: the agent's move;
    - #1.2 has no messages: nobody's move.

    Once the agent answers in #1.1, it is your move there.

    All of the rule is in one function:

    thread.waiting(r) decides it, and nothing else does: in an open review, every
    conversation whose move is the agent's. No list of waiting conversations is stored.
    show.lua calls it for each open review, and sending a message calls it too, to decide

    walking and accepting never affect it. An answer you have not read yet is still your
    move, not the agent's.

           32h ago  ●  Why does show.lua skip closed reviews, rather than list them last?

△  Because nothing in a closed review can wait for the agent, so listing one
   last would always show 0 waiting. thread.waiting returns an empty list
   for any review that is not open, and show.lua prints what it returns. Its
   own branch for closed adds two small things. A closed review is not
   counted among the open ones, and naming one prints that it is closed
   instead of an empty header.

   Closing a review is you saying you are done with it, the way closing a
   thread makes all its conversations nobody's move (#1.3). The one case
   that looks like lost work is a question you asked before closing the
   review, with no answer yet. Closing withdrew it. Anything you write after
   closing does reach the agent, because send opens the review again before
   it does anything else.

   The worked case is in tests/skill_spec.lua. A closed review shut has one
   thread holding only your message. show.lua lists nothing from it and
   counts one open review, the other one in the test. show.lua shut prints
   review shut is closed: nothing in it waits.

   I'd keep it. If an unanswered question should not slip by when you close
   a review, the close itself is the place to catch it. When stage 6 builds
   that action, it can say how many questions have no answer before it
   closes. That one is your call.

==== #4 whole
◇ #4  Read and walked follow the step's quote   HOW IT WORKS   1 step for you

    A step is known by its chunk's file and quoted line, or by its title when it is on no
    line, and that is hashed into its key. Walked marks and a step's read count use this
    key. The agent can therefore move, reorder or retitle a step that is on code, and you
    keep both marks.

    A worked case, which I ran in a scratch repository. A step on function M.get(t, k) was
    walked, then I changed its line to 40 and its title to something else. It was still
    walked, because neither the line number nor the title is in the key. Two steps quoting
    end on lines 4 and 7 of one file got the keys d16d84b5d77447f2 and d16d84b5d77447f2.2.
    The order of the steps tells them apart, not their line numbers.

    The key fails in two cases, both in #4.6.

==== #5 whole
◇ #5  Accepted follows the code   HOW IT WORKS 

    Accepting a chunk stores a key made from its code as it is now, under its thread. When
    the code changes, the key changes, and the chunk is back to accept. On a hunk of the
    review's diff, the key is the whole hunk's. On plain code, it covers the chunk's own
    lines, and a chunk with no lines is one line.

    Two cases I ran in scratch repositories, each a check below:

    - A diff that changes lines 3 and 4, one hunk. Accepting a chunk on line 3 also accepts
      one on line 4, since both have the key h911204fe4c47ec2b. Editing line 4 again makes
      line 3's chunk no longer accepted.
    - Plain code, function f(), with a one-line chunk and a three-line chunk on it, both
      accepted. After the body changes, the three-line chunk is back to accept and the
      one-line chunk is still accepted.
